What this means

Exploration guide
  • The p-value is the probability of a sample mean at least as extreme as the one observed, assuming H₀ is true — not the probability that H₀ is true.
  • We reject H₀ exactly when the p-value < α, i.e. when the observed mean falls in the red rejection region.
  • Raise α and watch the rejection region grow: rejecting becomes easier, but the Type I error rate rises with it.
  • Move the true mean away from the null and increase n: power rises and β shrinks. This is why larger samples detect smaller effects.
  • “Fail to reject” is not the same as “accept H₀” — a low-power test often fails to reject even when H₀ is false.
