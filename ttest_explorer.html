Exploration Guide & Learning Objectives

▼
  • Observe the Distributions: See how the overlap between the two normal curves changes when you move the population means ( vs ) further apart. The less overlap, the easier it is to detect a statistically significant difference.
  • Power of Sample Size: Change the sample sizes ( and ). Notice that the physical shape of the population distributions above does not change, but the calculated t-statistic increases and the p-value dramatically shrinks. More data provides higher confidence!
  • Critical Regions: On the bottom plot, the green line represents your calculated t-statistic, while the red shaded areas represent the critical regions (). If the green line falls inside the red shading, your result is statistically significant.
  • Unequal Variances: Load the "Unequal Variances" preset to see what happens when standard deviations are wildly different. Notice how the "Assumptions Check" panel catches this and uses Welch's adjustment automatically.
