End of life forecasting with the Weibull distribution

The Weibull distribution models how failure probability changes with age, capturing the wear and degradation of physical assets. It is the standard tool in reliability engineering and material flow analysis. In this applet the asset is a vehicle cohort: as cars age, the survival function tracks how many enter the scrap stream each year, forecasting the recovery of Critical Raw Materials (CRMs) to support the Dutch Raw Materials Strategy (Grondstoffenstrategie) and recyclers such as ARN (Auto Recycling Nederland).

Why the Weibull distribution?

Most durable assets exhibit failure that accelerates with age: a car that is 20 years old is far more likely to be scrapped than one that is 5 years old. A constant-rate (exponential) model cannot represent this pattern. The Weibull distribution does, through its shape parameter :

  • α > 1: hazard increases with age (wear; typical of vehicles)
  • α = 1: constant hazard (equivalent to the exponential distribution)
  • α < 1: hazard decreases with age (early defects dominate)

The scale parameter is the characteristic lifespan: the age by which 63% of any cohort has been scrapped.
